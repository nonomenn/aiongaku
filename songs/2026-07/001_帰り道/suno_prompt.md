# Suno Prompt：帰り道

## Style Prompt（英語・長文）

Genre: Japanese acoustic pop ballad, mid-tempo, coming-of-age/school-life love song. Rooted in acoustic singer-songwriter tradition with subtle orchestral pop touches — think intimate bedroom-pop warmth blended with soft cinematic strings, not glossy or overproduced.

Mood / Emotional arc: The song moves through four emotional stages like a slow exhale held over a walk home. It opens with a calm, comfortable everyday feeling — like two people falling into an unspoken rhythm without needing words. Through the pre-chorus it shifts into a quiet restlessness, like watching the sunset move faster than you want it to. The chorus blooms into a bittersweet ache — a wish half-spoken, like wanting to hold onto ten more minutes before reality returns. The bridge tightens into a throat-caught hesitation, words stuck unsaid, like the pause right before a confession. The last chorus releases into quiet courage and warmth — like the streetlights suddenly looking a little warmer than before, a small brave step taken in a near-whisper, never shouted.

Tempo: 74 BPM walking-pace ballad, with a barely perceptible forward lean toward 78 BPM feel just before the final chorus — like footsteps quickening without realizing it.

Key: D Major with Dorian/Aeolian coloring through the verses and pre-chorus for a bittersweet undertone, modulating up a whole step to E Major for the Last Chorus — like the same street suddenly looking slightly different in the evening light.

Vocals: Young female vocal, around 17 years old in tone — breathy, unpolished, intimate, never overly trained-sounding. Verse 1 is almost spoken, restrained and natural like a quiet conversation. Pre-chorus introduces a slight vocal tremble, an undercurrent of nervous urgency. Chorus opens the emotion but never belts — the ache seeps through rather than being pushed out. Verse 2 brightens momentarily, like a smile breaking through mid-sentence. The bridge features breath-caught pauses, as if words are stopping in the throat. Last Chorus carries a newfound core strength and openness after the modulation, more sustained and soaring, yet the final line "明日もきみと歩きたい" stays a whispered strength — emotionally the loudest moment of the song, but never screamed, like a secret finally spoken aloud.

Instrumentation: Acoustic guitar fingerpicked arpeggios as the harmonic backbone throughout, joined by soft piano from the pre-chorus onward. Strings (thin layered cello and violin) enter first in the chorus, adding emotional weight without density. Percussion is minimal — brushed drums or cajon-style soft hits only in the chorus and last chorus, completely absent in verses and bridge to preserve breathing space. A single glockenspiel/bell-like accent appears once in the last chorus, like the glint of a vending machine's light in the dusk. No electronic drums, no synths, no heavy bass — entirely organic, warm, close-mic'd acoustic textures.

Structure & dynamics: Intro (guitar arpeggio + wordless humming, like the first steps out of the school gate) → Verse 1 (minimal guitar-only, spoken-word intimacy) → Pre-Chorus (piano and strings gently entering, still no drums, rising tension via descending bassline) → Chorus (full but soft arrangement — strings, brushed percussion, first emotional bloom) → Verse 2 (arrangement pulls back toward Verse 1 lightness but retains a faint string residue, brighter emotional color) → Bridge (drums drop out completely, piano and sustained string tones only, tension of unspoken words) → Last Chorus (modulation up a whole step to E Major, full instrumentation returns thicker — layered strings, soft percussion, one glockenspiel accent, emotional peak) → Outro (guitar arpeggio and humming motif return in the new key, quietly settling, mirroring the intro but subtly transformed).

Production: Warm, close, organic acoustic production — like a live room recording with natural room tone, not a hyper-compressed pop mix. Dynamics should breathe; the loudest moment (Last Chorus) should still leave space and air, never crushing the mix into flatness. Vocal sits intimately upfront, slightly dry with a touch of natural room reverb. Strings and glockenspiel are treated as delicate color, not walls of sound. The ending should trail into near-silence, preserving lingering emotional afterglow rather than a hard stop.
