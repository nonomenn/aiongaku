# Suno Prompt：帰り道

## Style Prompt（英語・長文）

Genre: Japanese acoustic pop ballad, mid-tempo shoujo-manga-esque coming-of-age love song, organic and intimate — within the acoustic/ballad pop space (no EDM, no trap, no hip-hop elements).

Mood / Emotional arc: The song moves through four distinct emotional stages like a slow sunset walk home. It begins with a calm, comfortable daily-routine warmth (like a quiet, familiar feeling), drifts into a nervous, fluttering unease as dusk falls (like a heartbeat-quickening feeling), swells into a bittersweet longing to stretch the moment a little longer (like holding your breath before saying something feeling), and finally resolves into a quiet, trembling courage as the protagonist takes a small brave step forward (like finally exhaling after being brave feeling). The emotional journey should feel understated throughout — never bombastic, always intimate, like a diary entry set to music.

Tempo: 72 BPM, 4/4 time signature, Key: F Major (modulating up a whole step to G Major in the final chorus section).

Vocals: Female vocal, late-teens to early-20s tone, natural and unpolished with a slightly breathy quality — not overly trained or run through heavy vocal processing. In the verses, she sings conversationally, almost speaking-singing, with restrained inflection, like someone casually chatting beside a friend. In the pre-chorus, her breath becomes subtly unsteady, adding a sense of nervous anticipation. In the chorus, she sings "as if wishing for something" — never belting, never over-extending note endings, keeping a gentle, yearning restraint. In the final chorus, on the line "tomorrow too, let's walk home together" her voice lifts just slightly with newfound resolve, and the immediately following final line is sung almost in a whisper, trailing off tenderly to let the emotion land softly.

Instrumentation: Acoustic fingerpicked guitar arpeggios as the harmonic backbone throughout, joined by soft piano chords that enter subtly from the pre-chorus onward to reinforce the harmony. A distant railway crossing bell sound appears only in the intro and outro as an ambient environmental texture, grounding the song in a specific place and time (a dusk commute). Brushed, soft percussion enters starting at the first chorus, kept light and textural rather than driving. The chorus introduces a restrained string pad that gently widens the emotional space without overwhelming the acoustic core, alongside a bass line that for the first time plays a clear, singable melodic line. In the final chorus, after the key change, the strings thicken and shadow the vocal melody more closely, the piano adds delicate high-register octave embellishments, and the acoustic guitar strums with more rhythmic drive for forward momentum — all while keeping the overall mix restrained so the emotional afterglow is never crushed by loudness.

Structure & dynamics: Intro (ambient guitar arpeggio + distant train crossing sound, 0:00-0:12) → Verse 1 (intimate, conversational, sparse arrangement, 0:12-0:40) → Pre-Chorus (rising tension, breath grows unsteady, piano enters, 0:40-1:00) → Chorus (opens with restrained volume and instrumentation, then blooms with strings and bass on the second refrain "just ten more minutes," 1:00-1:30) → Verse 2 (returns to intimate restraint, 1:30-1:55) → Pre-Chorus 2 (1:55-2:15) → Bridge (introspective, slightly hesitant, building quietly toward the key change, 2:15-2:45) → Last Chorus (key change from F to G Major happens right as the emotional turning point "I said it" lands, fuller instrumentation, a brief dynamic drop-out/breath before "tomorrow too, let's walk home together," then swells back in, 2:45-3:30) → Outro (acoustic guitar arpeggio fades with the train crossing sound returning, 3:30-3:50).

Production: Warm, organic, slightly lo-fi acoustic production feel — like a close-mic'd bedroom recording, not glossy or maximalist. Mix should preserve dynamic headroom and breathing space rather than pushing loudness; the emotional payoff comes from restraint and air between instruments, not density or volume. Reverb should feel natural and spacious (like an evening street, not a studio hall). The final two lines of the last chorus should be mixed with just acoustic guitar and vocal, almost dry and close, so the whispered delivery feels private and unguarded before dissolving into the ambient outro.
